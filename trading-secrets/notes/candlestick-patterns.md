# Candlestick Patterns

Candlestick patterns are formations on a price chart that are used in [[technical-analysis]] to predict future price movements. Each candlestick represents a specific time period and shows the open, high, low, and close prices for that period.

## Importance

- **Market Psychology**: Patterns can provide insights into the sentiment and psychology of market participants (bulls vs. bears).
- **Reversal and Continuation Signals**: They can signal potential trend reversals or the continuation of an existing trend.
- **Entry and Exit Points**: Used to identify high-probability entry and exit points for trades.

## Example: Hammer Pattern (ASCII)

A Hammer is a bullish reversal pattern that forms during a downtrend. It has a small body at the top and a long lower wick, indicating that sellers pushed the price down, but buyers came in strongly to close the price near the open.

```
      -------  (Open/Close)
         |
         |     (Small Body)
      -------  (Open/Close)
         |
         |
         |     (Long Lower Wick)
         |
         .
```
