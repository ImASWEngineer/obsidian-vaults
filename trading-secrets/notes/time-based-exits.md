# Time-Based Exits

A time-based exit strategy involves closing a trade after a predetermined period, regardless of whether a [[profit-targets]] or [[stop-loss]] has been hit. This approach is a component of a disciplined [[trading-plan]] and helps manage exposure and opportunity cost.

## Purpose

-   **Opportunity Cost**: Frees up capital and mental energy from trades that are not moving as expected, allowing the trader to seek new opportunities.
-   **Risk Management**: Limits exposure to overnight or weekend risk, or to periods of high uncertainty.
-   **Discipline**: Enforces [[trading-discipline]] by preventing a trader from holding onto a position indefinitely in the hope of a favorable move.

## Common Applications

-   **End of Day**: Day traders often close all positions before the market closes to avoid overnight risk.
-   **Fixed Duration**: Exiting a trade after a set number of minutes, hours, or days if it hasn't reached its target or stop.
-   **News Events**: Exiting before major economic announcements or company-specific news that could introduce high volatility.

Time-based exits are particularly relevant for [[day-trading-strategies]] where positions are typically closed within the same trading session.
