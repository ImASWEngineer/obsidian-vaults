# Leverage Management

Leverage in trading refers to using borrowed capital to increase the potential returns of an investment. While it can amplify profits, it also significantly magnifies potential losses. Effective leverage management is a critical component of [[risk-management]] and is essential for long-term survival in the markets.

## How Leverage Works

Leverage is typically expressed as a ratio (e.g., 1:10, 1:500). A 1:10 leverage means that for every $1 of your own capital, you can control $10 worth of assets. This is often facilitated through margin accounts provided by brokers.

## Risks Associated with High Leverage

-   **Amplified Losses**: A small unfavorable price movement can lead to substantial losses, potentially exceeding initial capital.
-   **Margin Calls**: If losses reduce your account equity below a certain threshold, your broker may issue a margin call, requiring you to deposit more funds or face forced liquidation of positions.
-   **Emotional Stress**: The rapid swings in profit and loss due to high leverage can lead to poor [[emotional-control]] and [[revenge-trading]].

## Responsible Leverage Management

Prudent [[risk-per-trade]] and [[position-sizing]] are key to managing leverage. Traders should only use leverage that aligns with their [[risk-tolerance]] and ensures that even a series of losing trades does not lead to a margin call or significant [[drawdowns]]. It's often recommended to risk only a small percentage of capital per trade, regardless of the available leverage.
