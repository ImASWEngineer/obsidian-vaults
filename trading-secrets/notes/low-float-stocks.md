## Summary
This note defines "float" and explains why low-float stocks are often targeted by momentum day traders.

## What is Float?
Float is the number of a company's shares that are available for trading on the open market. It's calculated by taking the total outstanding shares and subtracting any closely-held or restricted shares (e.g., those held by insiders, governments).

- A low float (typically under 20 million shares) means there is a limited supply of shares. When a [[catalyst-for-stock-movement]] creates high demand, this supply/demand imbalance can lead to rapid, volatile price increases, which is what momentum traders seek.
