## Summary
The Risk-Reward Ratio (R:R) is a fundamental concept in trading that compares the potential loss of a trade to its potential profit. It is calculated by dividing the amount of money a trader risks by the potential profit they expect to make.

## Calculation
**Risk-Reward Ratio = (Potential Profit) / (Potential Loss)**

For example, if you risk $100 to potentially make $300, your R:R is 3:1.

## Importance
- **Positive Expectancy**: A favorable R:R (e.g., 2:1 or higher) means you can have a win rate below 50% and still be profitable over time.
- **Discipline**: Forces traders to define their [[stop-loss-strategies]] and [[profit-targets]] before entering a trade.
- **Capital Preservation**: Encourages taking trades where the upside significantly outweighs the downside.
