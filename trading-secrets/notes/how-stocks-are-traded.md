## Summary
This note explains the basic mechanics of how stocks are bought and sold in the market through exchanges, brokers, and different order types.

## Core Components
1.  **Exchanges**: Centralized marketplaces (e.g., NYSE, NASDAQ) where buyers and sellers are matched. They provide a regulated environment for trading.
2.  **Brokers**: Firms that act as intermediaries, providing individuals and institutions with access to the exchanges. See [[choosing-a-day-trading-broker]].
3.  **Bid-Ask Spread**: The difference between the highest price a buyer is willing to pay for a stock (the bid) and the lowest price a seller is willing to accept (the ask). This spread is a key component of market liquidity.
4.  **Order Types**:
    - **Market Order**: Buys or sells immediately at the best available current price.
    - **Limit Order**: Buys or sells at a specific price or better.
    - **Stop Order**: Becomes a market order once a specific price (the "stop price") is reached. Used for risk management.
