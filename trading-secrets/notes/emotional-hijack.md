# Emotional Hijack in Trading

An emotional hijack, a concept from Daniel Goleman's "Emotional Intelligence," refers to a sudden and overwhelming emotional response that is disproportionate to the situation and leads to irrational decision-making. In trading, this often manifests as a rapid shift from rational analysis to impulsive actions driven by fear, greed, or frustration.

## Triggers in Trading

-   **Sudden Losses**: A large or unexpected loss can trigger intense frustration or anger, leading to [[revenge-trading]].
-   **Rapid Gains**: Euphoria from quick profits can lead to overconfidence and [[overtrading]], or taking on excessive [[leverage-management]].
-   **[[fomo-in-trading]]**: The fear of missing out on a perceived opportunity can lead to impulsive entries into trades that don't fit the [[trading-plan]].

## Impact on Trading

When a trader is emotionally hijacked, their ability to think clearly, adhere to their [[trading-discipline]], and execute their [[trading-plan]] is severely compromised. This can result in significant financial losses and undermine long-term profitability. Developing strong [[emotional-control]] and self-awareness is key to recognizing and mitigating emotional hijacks.
