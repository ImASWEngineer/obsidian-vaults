# Pullback Trading

Pullback trading is a [[day-trading-strategies]] that involves entering a trade in the direction of the prevailing [[trend-analysis]] after a temporary counter-trend movement (a "pullback" or "retracement"). Instead of chasing a stock that is already moving strongly, traders wait for a brief dip in an uptrend or a bounce in a downtrend, aiming for a more favorable entry price.

## Key Concepts

-   **Trend Identification**: The first step is to identify a clear, established uptrend or downtrend.
-   **Pullback**: A temporary reversal against the main trend. In an uptrend, this is a dip; in a downtrend, it's a rally.
-   **Support/Resistance**: Pullbacks often find support at previous [[support-and-resistance]] levels, [[moving-averages]], or Fibonacci retracement levels.

## Strategy Overview

Traders look for [[pullback-entries]] when the price pulls back to a key level (like a moving average or a previous resistance-turned-support) and then shows signs of resuming the original trend. This strategy aims to reduce risk by entering at a better price point than chasing a breakout.

## Risk Management

[[stop-loss]] orders are typically placed below the support level (for long trades) or above the resistance level (for short trades) where the pullback is expected to reverse. [[profit-targets]] are set based on the continuation of the main trend.
