# Relative Strength Index (RSI)

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum oscillator used in [[technical-analysis]] that measures the speed and change of price movements. It oscillates between 0 and 100 and is typically used to identify overbought or oversold conditions in the market.

## Key Concepts

-   **Calculation**: RSI is calculated using the average gains and losses over a specified period, usually 14 periods (e.g., 14 days or 14 candles).
-   **Overbought/Oversold Levels**:
    -   Readings above 70 generally indicate an overbought condition, suggesting a potential reversal or pullback.
    -   Readings below 30 generally indicate an oversold condition, suggesting a potential bounce or reversal upwards.
-   **Divergence**: When the price of an asset makes a new high or low, but the RSI does not confirm it with a corresponding new high or low, it's called divergence. This can signal a weakening trend and a potential reversal.
-   **Trend Confirmation**: RSI can also be used to confirm a [[trend-analysis]]. For example, during an uptrend, RSI tends to stay above 30 and often reaches 70. During a downtrend, it tends to stay below 70 and often reaches 30.

RSI is a valuable tool for assessing the strength of price action and identifying potential turning points, often used in conjunction with other [[technical-analysis]] tools.
