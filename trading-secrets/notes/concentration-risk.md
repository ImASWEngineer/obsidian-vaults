# Concentration Risk

Concentration risk refers to the potential for losses arising from an excessive exposure to a single asset, sector, industry, or geographical region. In trading, it means putting too much capital into one particular stock or a small group of highly correlated stocks.

## Key Aspects

-   **Single Stock Exposure**: Holding a disproportionately large percentage of your trading capital in one company's stock.
-   **Sector Concentration**: Investing heavily in a single industry sector (e.g., technology, energy) which can be vulnerable to sector-specific downturns.
-   **Strategy Concentration**: Relying too heavily on one specific [[day-trading-strategies]] without diversification into other approaches.

## Impact on Trading

High concentration risk can lead to significant [[drawdowns]] if the concentrated position moves unfavorably. It increases the volatility of a trading account and can lead to catastrophic losses if the single asset or sector experiences a sharp decline.

## Mitigation

Effective [[risk-management]] involves diversifying across multiple assets, sectors, and strategies to reduce concentration risk. This is closely related to [[position-sizing]] and ensuring that no single trade or asset can severely impact overall capital.
