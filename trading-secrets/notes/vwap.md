# Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP)

The Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) is a trading benchmark used by traders that gives the average price a security has traded at throughout the day, based on both volume and price. It is an important indicator for institutional traders and is often used as a reference point for intraday trading decisions.

## Key Concepts

-   **Calculation**: VWAP is calculated by adding the dollar amount of every transaction (price multiplied by number of shares traded) and then dividing by the total shares traded for the day.
-   **Intraday Focus**: VWAP is primarily an intraday indicator, resetting at the beginning of each trading day.
-   **Interpretation**:
    -   If the price is above VWAP, it suggests that buyers are in control and the stock is trading above its average price for the day.
    -   If the price is below VWAP, it suggests that sellers are in control and the stock is trading below its average price for the day.
-   **Support and Resistance**: VWAP often acts as a dynamic [[support-and-resistance]] level. Traders might look to buy when the price pulls back to VWAP in an uptrend or short when it rallies to VWAP in a downtrend.

VWAP is a powerful tool for [[technical-analysis]] as it provides a more accurate representation of a stock's average price by incorporating [[volume-analysis]].
