# Liquidity Risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that a given security or asset cannot be traded quickly enough in the market without causing a significant price change and loss of capital. In day trading, this often refers to the ability to enter or exit a position at a desired price.

## Types of Liquidity Risk

-   **Market Liquidity Risk**: The risk that an asset cannot be sold due to a lack of demand in the market. This is common in thinly traded stocks.
-   **Funding Liquidity Risk**: The risk that a trader will not have sufficient cash to meet their financial obligations (e.g., margin calls).

## Impact on Trading

High [[liquidity-risk]] can lead to wider bid-ask spreads, increased slippage, and difficulty in executing [[stop-loss]] orders effectively, especially during volatile periods. Traders often focus on highly liquid assets to minimize this risk, which is a crucial consideration in [[risk-management]].
