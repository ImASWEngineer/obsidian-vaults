# Drawdowns

A drawdown is the peak-to-trough decline in an investment, trading account, or fund over a specific period. It is typically quoted as a percentage of the peak value. Drawdowns are a key metric in [[risk-management]] and performance analysis, indicating the magnitude of losses from a previous high.

## Types of Drawdowns

-   **Absolute Drawdown**: The largest decline from the initial capital.
-   **Maximum Drawdown**: The largest percentage drop from a peak equity value to a subsequent trough before a new peak is achieved. This is often considered the most important drawdown metric.

## Significance

Understanding and managing drawdowns is crucial for a trader's longevity. Large drawdowns require disproportionately larger gains to recover. For example, a 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain to return to the original capital level. Effective [[risk-management]] strategies, including strict [[risk-per-trade]] limits and [[daily-loss-limits]], aim to minimize the frequency and severity of drawdowns.

Monitoring drawdowns helps in assessing the volatility and risk associated with a particular [[trading-strategy]].
