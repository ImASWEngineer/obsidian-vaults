# Risk Per Trade

Risk per trade refers to the maximum amount of capital a trader is willing to lose on any single trade. It is a fundamental concept in [[risk-management]] and a cornerstone of a robust [[trading-plan]].

## Importance

- **Capital Preservation**: By defining a fixed risk per trade, traders protect their overall capital from significant depletion due to a single losing position.
- **Emotional Control**: It helps in maintaining [[emotional-control]] by quantifying potential losses upfront, reducing the impact of fear and greed during live trading.
- **Consistency**: Promotes consistent [[position-sizing]] and prevents over-leveraging.

## Calculation

Typically, risk per trade is expressed as a small percentage of the total trading capital (e.g., 0.5% to 2%). For example, if a trader has a $10,000 account and sets a 1% risk per trade, they are willing to lose a maximum of $100 on any given trade. This amount then dictates the [[stop-loss]] placement and the number of shares or contracts to trade.

This concept is closely related to [[daily-loss-limits]] and managing overall [[drawdowns]].
