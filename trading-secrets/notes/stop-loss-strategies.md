## Summary
A stop-loss is an order placed with a broker to buy or sell a security once it reaches a certain price, designed to limit a trader's potential loss on a position. Effective stop-loss placement is fundamental to [[risk-management]].

## Types of Stop-Losses
- **Hard Stop**: A predefined price level at which you will exit the trade, regardless of emotion or market noise. This is the most recommended type for day traders.
- **Mental Stop**: A stop-loss that is not placed with the broker but is held in the trader's mind. Highly susceptible to [[emotional-hijack]] and not recommended for beginners.
- **Trailing Stop**: A stop-loss that adjusts as the price moves in your favor, locking in profits while allowing for further gains.

## Placement Considerations
- **Technical Levels**: Place stops below support, above resistance, or based on chart patterns.
- **Volatility**: Adjust stop distance based on the stock's average true range (ATR) to avoid being stopped out by normal market fluctuations.
- **Risk Per Trade**: Ensure your stop-loss, combined with your [[position-sizing]], adheres to your maximum acceptable [[risk-per-trade]].
